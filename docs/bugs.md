[Known issues](../README.md#known-issues) in the README: those are limitations
we accept and document, these are bugs we intend to remove or have removed.

running count (B3, B4, B6, B8, B9 and B11 have no entry here).

  well).

Each entry has the same parts. **Seen** is what a player or operator saw, with
the date. **Cause** is the evidence and how far it carries; a hypothesis is
marked as one, and nothing here is a diagnosis until it has been reproduced.
**Fix** says what changed and in which modules: this file names modules, not
commit hashes, because the public tree is one squashed commit per release and
cannot resolve them. **Tests** names the test files and tests that pin it.
**Status** gives the standing and its date. An open entry adds **Where to
start**.

## B1 — A cheap divination card was PRICED at a fortune, and where that came from is still unknown

- **Seen:** 2026-08-10, 24h window, Allflame league, from a screenshot of the History page: **The Twilight Moon**, quantity **+1**, recorded at **+55.2 div** — at that hour's rate (212 c/div) roughly **11,700 chaos** for a card that trades at about **1 chaos**. Two more cards looked suspicious by the same measure: **The Demoness** +1 → +7.8 div, **Enmity's Embrace** −1 → −12.9 div.
- **Cause:** two halves. The guard hole is reproduced: `apply()` in `price-guard.mjs` walked `curMap`, looked up `prevMap.get(name)` and `continue`d when there was none, so an item ARRIVING had no previous unit price and nothing for `GUARD_FACTOR` to measure against; every event above is a ±1 quantity change, an arrival or a departure. The price itself is unexplained: nothing recorded that night says whether the 11,700c came from Faustus below its volume floor or from a poe.ninja line that matched the wrong row, and the container's log is gone (the same dead end as the Scavenger case). It cannot be answered from the repository.
- **Fix:** the guard judges arrivals too: its own memory of prices it has accepted per name, plus a share-and-divine bar that withholds an unknown big arrival until it has stood. The price **source** of every item survives the valuation and is named in the log line (`docs/pricing-logic.md`, "History: what a recorded point is protected against"). Since 2026-11-03 the memory and every running hold are kept next to the series in `networth-history-guard.json` (`networth/guard-state.mjs`), for exactly as long as they would have lived in memory, and only for names the items sidecar does not already carry; those come back from the sidecar at start. A restart's downtime counts like a point without a price, so an update neither releases a hold nor starts it over, and does not reopen the window (the circumstance of the Scavenger night). That night itself cannot be brought back.
- **Limits of the guard, left on purpose** (found by review before it ran on the container; each fix would need a number no measurement supports, stacked on the 20-divine arrival floor that is already declared "a judgement call bounded by the one measured case", and a second guessed number hides which of them was wrong when the next incident arrives):
  1. The arrival bar does not exist below its floor, on any account. The floor is absolute and AND-ed with the share, so a 19-divine arrival passes unexamined on a 5-divine account as on a 94-divine one. Scaling the floor by the share and OR-ing it with the previous total both misfire at the bottom of the range (on an account worth a tenth of a divine, ordinary league-start drops become "suspicious"); a separate small-account bar is the third option. Deciding needs a second observed case, and there is one in total.
  2. Several arrivals under the floor in one point are judged one by one: ten bogus 19-divine names are ten passes, not one 190-divine event. Judging the *sum* would withhold every name of a legitimate inventory dump for six hours.
- **Remaining gap** (older than the guard-state fix, not deliberate): when the items sidecar does not match the series' newest point after a restart (its point never reached the series, retention dropped it, or the sidecar write after the point's line did not land — the process stopped in between, or the write failed, which is not logged), `history.mjs` adopts no breakdown and the first point afterwards is recorded without the brake: the guard only runs against a previous breakdown. From the second point on it runs, with the restored memory.
- **Scope:** The Demoness (7.8 div) and Enmity's Embrace (12.9 div) from the screenshot are below the arrival bar's divine floor and are not caught. Neither was shown to be wrong, and suppressing arrivals that small for six hours on suspicion costs more than it buys.
- **Tests:** `price-guard.test.mjs` reproduces the Twilight Moon arrival; it failed before the change and passes after it.
- **Status:** open, cause unknown (reported from live use 2026-08-10). The recorded series is guarded since 2026-08-11, the valuation is not.
- **Where to start:** when the brake fires again, take the `[networth]` line off the container. It names the item, both prices, the quantity, the share and the source: `source faustus` points at that hour's digest, `source exchange:DivinationCard` at poe.ninja's card overview line for that name. Since 2026-11-03 the line also says whether the valuation already doubted the price (`source faustus [uncertain: thin-market]`). Until one of those two is in hand, anything about the cause is a guess. Code: `backend/v2/networth/kinds.mjs` (the divination-card branch, Faustus first, then the poe.ninja card exchange overview), `backend/v2/networth/valuator.mjs` (`exchangeSingle`, the volume floors), `docs/pricing-logic.md` (how cards are valued, and the confidence floors).

## B2 — A momentary drop in the curve with no matching event

- **Seen:** on the screenshot of B1 (2026-08-10), a single sharp dip just before 08:31 PM, down from the ~94 div baseline and back within one recording interval, with no entry in What moved (that list's threshold was 1 div). From live use on 2026-08-14, on an untouched stash: seven events over six hours in mirror pairs, mirror images to the decimal — 09:37 The Card Sharp +3 +12.6 c / 10:16 −3 −12.6 c; 05:37 Weeping Essence of Contempt +7 +18.8 c / 06:16 −7 −18.8 c; 05:37 Runegraft of Fury +1 +11 c / 10:16 −1 −12.4 c — and, via `GET /networth-events`, one event over 18 points (about 50 minutes) with 53 lines: Divine Orb −6 −1155.6 c, Chromatic Orb −80 −195.5 c, Chaos Orb −133 −133 c, Enkindling Orb −35 −39.8 c, Grand Eldritch Ember −6 −69.7 c, Simulacrum Splinter +71 +12.5 c, Orb of Alteration +18, Sepia Oil +25. The negatives were the whole currency tab and the positives the whole of another: a stash tab went missing, not an item. The page says: "Events are stock movements — what came in and what went out. Price moves are in the curve, not in the list."
- **Cause:** four mechanisms, each fixed; none is shown to have produced the 08:31 PM dip. What moved groups points into buckets BEFORE applying its threshold (hourly on the 24h range), so an out-and-back inside one bucket cancels: an empty list is no evidence that the stock did not move.
  1. *Partial sync.* `history.append()` wrote a point for whatever `networthSnapshot()` summed (the union of the per-tab store), the refresher fills that store ONE TAB PER TICK, and the only condition on a point was the one-per-minute throttle. A related wipe: `pruneTabs(user, [])` deleted every stored tab on an empty tab list (closed 2026-08-11; the warning line in place since that day did not fire once in a full day of production logging). Whether this produced the dip cannot be told from the repository.
  2. *Release booked as a price move* (found by review, closed before it ran): the guard's release of a withheld arrival was dropped by `events()` and read as nothing by `stockMove()`, so a genuine 25-divine find stepped into the curve unexplained six hours later. Not a candidate: the withholding did not exist that evening.
  3. *A lost price read as stock leaving.* The stored delta was built from `foldItems(snapshot.items)`, which holds priced rows only (`aggregate.mjs` puts the unpriced into `snapshot.unpriced`), and `buildDelta` read a name in the previous map but not the current one as `dq = -q0`, the whole stock leaving at its price. Which gate dropped those prices is not answerable, and the fix does not depend on it; candidates: `POE_FAUSTUS_MAX_AGE_HOURS` (6 by default), `MIN_EXCHANGE_UNITS` / `MIN_EXCHANGE_TURNOVER_C` in `valuator.mjs` (essences sit at that floor, a Runegraft line fails it), Faustus `QUOTE_VOL_MIN` / `THIN_X_UNITS` per hourly digest. A candidate for the 08:31 PM dip and the first that explains a dip plus an empty list; not established that it fired (the age gate did not exist then, the volume floors did). Second half: `aggregate.mjs` capped `snapshot.unpriced` at 100 names ranked by piece count and the recorder was fed that list, so a name ranked below it fell back to the old reading; after the first fix phantom rows per block went from four to six down to two (thinly traded survivors).
  4. *A whole tab.* Ordinary tabs took `Array.isArray(j?.items) ? j.items : []`; folder tabs did `catch { … return [] }` for anything but auth and ratelimit, and `finishFolderWalk` handed up items when a sub-tab answered 200 without an items array (`folder-walk.mjs` counted those, `withItemsArray`). The chain: `refresher.mjs` `step()` writes the `fetchTab` result through `store.setTab` (EMPTY, fresh `syncedAt`) → `coverageOf` counts the tab covered → `server.mjs` `recordNetworthHistory` sums the store → `history.append` writes a point with `syncComplete: true` → `buildDelta` books `dq = -q0`; the next turn books it backwards, and the fresh `syncedAt` puts the tab at the back of the round-robin, a full lap (tens of minutes) away.
- **Besides the four mechanisms, a rate:** until 2026-08-14 the trend line plotted chaos (`history-chart.js` drew `p[1]`). Since 2026-08-15 `displaySeries()` in `history-chart.js` converts every point to divine with the rate stored on that point (a point without one borrows the nearest), once one point in the window carries a rate. A wrong rate for one interval is a candidate for any dip today, and nothing recorded says which rate a point carried.
- **Fix:**
  1. The refresher reports coverage of the live tab list and the recorder refuses a point until every tab has been walked; a tab that is never readable counts as walked, because its absence is then a constant rather than a dip. A stored tab is dropped only when two CONSECUTIVE tab lists agree it is gone (their intersection; disagreeing lists confirm neither, one whole list in between clears the proposal). A single hiccup costs a log line; there is no "how much shorter" threshold and no extra request. A tab genuinely deleted in game keeps showing its stored value for one more list interval.
  2. The release is booked as stock arriving at the point where the series can first count it (`withoutReleased` in `history.mjs`).
  3. 2026-08-14: `foldItems(items, unpriced)` folds unpriced names into the breakdown at value zero, `server.mjs` hands the list along. A name that loses its price has quantity delta 0 and its whole value change on the price side (curve, detail panel, no event list); a name in neither list is booked as outflow. No new threshold, no new number, and the split is not softened: the value change stays in the curve, not moved into the event list with a label. The cap is closed the same day: `scan.mjs` writes each tab's stored unpriced list uncapped (`unpricedMax: Infinity`), bounded by the tab's slots (priced rows were never capped); the server folds the stored lists account-wide for the recorder (`unpricedStock`, `{name, count}` per name), with nothing added to the snapshot. `snapshot.unpriced` stays the same 100-name display list, and the page states the pieces it does not list from `tabs[].unpricedPieces`. No schema break, no re-scan: tabs written before still carry at most 100 names, and completeness returns tab by tab over one round-robin lap.
  4. 2026-08-14: three states are told apart. Read with contents (unchanged); read and empty (`items: []` is stored and does not block); not known (a 200 without an items array, a folder sub-tab that does not answer or an unaddressable entry, an unexpected status) throws `kind: 'unread'`: the tab keeps its stored contents, nothing is written, no `onTabSynced`, no point is recorded from that step. `folderWalkComplete()` is the predicate for the folder case and `finishFolderWalk` throws rather than return a subset; tick-driven and loop-driven walks behave identically (B5). A 429 mid-walk keeps its sub-tab: the cursor goes back for `ratelimit` only, with no retry loop, every other error drops the whole walk, and a sub-tab that keeps answering 429 costs one request per cooldown. `pickStalest` takes the LATER of the stored `syncedAt` and the attempt stamp, so a failing tab does not pin the round-robin. `coverageOf` counts a tab covered once an attempt is stamped (the stamp is set before the request), deliberately, so a never-readable tab cannot stop the series. The recorder compares the set of tabs the snapshot was summed over (`createTabSetWatch` in `history.mjs`); on a change `append({ tabsChanged: true })` refuses exactly one point and forgets the previous breakdown (the watch updates on every look). `coverageOf` reports `tabsRead`, and the recorder logs once when it writes points over less than the whole account.
  5. Log lines (the log table in `docs/admin.md`): `[stash-client] … was NOT read in full`, `[networth-refresher] a tab of <user> was NOT read and is left as it was stored`, `[networth-history] the stash tabs <user> is summed over changed`, the pair `[networth-history] recording for <user> over N of M tabs` / `every tab of <user> reads again`, and the unchanged pause pair `no points for X until the sync covers the whole account` / `covered again`.
- **Tests:** `refresher.test.mjs`, "after a short tab list wiped the store, coverage reads incomplete again" (drives a wipe with two consecutive empty tab lists). `history.test.mjs` (the guard release). `history-unpriced.test.mjs`: a flicker produces no event, without the unpriced list the mirror pair comes back, four diff cases, a guard release is still inflow, a held item unpriced for one point does not end its hold; for the cap, a name below the 100 busiest records no movement, the capped list brings the phantom back, the page list keeps 100 names, the `server.mjs` call site is pinned. `aggregate.test.mjs`: the cap cuts the drawn list, not the stored one, and `unpricedStock` merges a name across tabs. `backend/v2/networth/tab-unread.test.mjs`: the empty write shape; a tab that does not answer writes, re-stamps, broadcasts and records nothing; a genuinely empty tab does not block; a failing tab does not pin the round-robin; a pruned tab and a first-read tab each cost one point and no phantom. `folder-walk.test.mjs` (complete vs merely finished, both shapes of incompleteness), `stash-client.test.mjs` (a 200 without an items array, a 404 on the folder endpoint, `items: []` still empty).
- **Status:** open for the original screenshot; all four mechanisms fixed (the fourth on 2026-08-14, the day it was reported). The fourth closes the reported 2026-08-14 events and is the strongest candidate for the 08:31 PM dip, still only a candidate. Neither 2026-08-14 pass (the lost price, the cap) has been shown to work on live data yet: counted off the recorded file, an untouched stash produced 2–6 movement rows per hour all through the night *before* either fix, and 4, 0 and 2 in the three hours after the first — the same range; the reading "four-to-six per block down to two" measured the block size, not the rate. Whether any mechanism fired on 2026-08-10 cannot be told from the repository. Recorded phantoms are not rewritten or marked: a stored row is byte-identical to a real sale, marking would need a guessed N hours, and they age out with the 15-day retention. Tab-sized phantoms are recognisable by eye — tens of names moving at once, in one direction, each by its entire stock, with the countermovement one lap later (Divine Orb −6 next to 52 others) — which is not a code rule.
- **Where to start:** a recorder refusal logs one `[networth-history]` line when it starts and one when recording resumes. If a dip recurs without either line, the remaining candidate is a single tab revalued and restored; observe it with `POE_NETWORTH_DEBUG=1` (one line per synced tab). For the 2026-08-14 repairs, the deciding measurement is several offline hours after both passes.

## B5 — One folder tab occupies the refresher for minutes (visible 2026-08-11, and the occupancy itself removed 2026-08-13)

- **Seen:** 2026-08-10, read live off the running container over `ws://…/events`: 18 tabs walked 30 s apart, `07:18:22Z` to `07:27:55Z`; then **nothing for ten minutes** — probes at `07:32:40Z`, `07:35:17Z` and `07:37:48Z` saw every `syncedAt` frozen, `networth-sync-status` said `state: "ok"` throughout, and the container log had no `[networth-refresher]` line at all. At `07:38:07Z` the `Maps` tab appeared with a fresh stamp and the 30 s cadence resumed (`Fragment` 07:38:52, `Essence` 07:39:22, …): a single `step()` had run for about ten minutes.
- **Cause:** folder tabs are fetched as a child list plus one request per child, each with a hard timeout and retries (`requestUrl()` and `withTimeout()` in `backend/v2/networth/stash-client.mjs`), and the whole walk ran inside one step. `tickOnce()` latches `busy = true` for the whole of `step()` and clears it in `finally`, with no watchdog, and nothing reported a running tick, so a long folder tab and a dead refresher looked the same. `busy` is global and the round-robin advances only when a step returns, so a folder tab also stopped the other USERS: for 913 s at a 24 s spacing, for the best part of an hour at the 80 s of an unauthenticated rule set (B16); a full round went from hours to days. The ten minutes of 2026-08-10 have two explanations and nothing in the material chooses between them: `delayFor()`'s reactive brake, which returns the whole window once a state counter reaches `limit-1` (slept capped at 90 s), or 31 merely slow responses, each allowed 30 s before the hard timeout. Neither was logged; the observation is compatible with the tool working as designed.
- **Fix:** 2026-08-11, visibility: `statusFor()` also answers `lastTab` (when a tab was last written for this user and which one, read from the store, so a restart cannot make it lie) and `current` (the step in flight: `phase`, `since`, and the tab name when the step belongs to this user; another user's step shows as busy but unnamed). Both ride `networth-sync-status` (see `backend/v2/frontend/PROTOCOL.md`). A step that holds the tick longer than `POE_NETWORTH_SLOW_STEP_MS` (default 120s) logs `[networth-refresher] still busy: …` **once**, and a second line when it returns. 2026-08-13, occupancy: the walk is plain data the refresher puts down between ticks (`networth/folder-walk.mjs`: `beginFolderWalk` / `stepFolderWalk` / `finishFolderWalk`), driven one request per tick by the refresher and in a loop by `stash-client.fetchTab` for callers without ticks (the one-shot scan). A folder tab is `1 + N` steps instead of one step of `1 + N` requests: same requests, same order, same pace, one per turn. A separate budget for folder tabs was rejected: it cannot make 1+N cheaper, only spread it over more ticks. The partial walk lives in the refresher's per-user memory next to `st.tabs` and `st.attempts`, NEVER in the store: a half-read folder tab there would be read as read by the snapshot, `tabList` and `coverageOf`, which is B7 with the tool doing it to itself; the previous complete contents of the tab stand until the walk finishes. `coverageOf` counts the tab from the walk's FIRST step, so B2's recorder gate is unaffected. A failure mid-walk drops the walk and writes nothing, and the tab is walked again from the beginning later; a **429 keeps** the walk and resumes after the cooldown. The tab-list refresh waits for a walk instead of interrupting one. For a folder tab the `still busy` / `finished after` pair does not fire; a closing `[networth-refresher] folder tab … N sub-tabs read over S steps in Ts` line and `folderTab` in the sync status ("12 of 37" between ticks) take its place, and B7's `yielded 0 items` line is emitted by the shared walk code. 2026-11-02: the Net Worth status counter reads `{recent}/{total} tabs synced in the last 10 min · oldest tab synced {mins} min ago` and invents no round duration, since the client knows neither the interval nor the number of users (`autosyncSummary()` in `backend/v2/frontend/networth-sync.js`). Deliberately not built: a watchdog that RELEASES `busy`. It would put a second request against GGG in flight next to the first and break the strict sequentiality every rate-limit decision in `stash-client.mjs` rests on; this project has a ban history, and a bound must end the *request*, not step around it. Since a step is one request, the longest a tick can hold `busy` is one derived spacing plus one request. Code: `backend/v2/networth/refresher.mjs` (`tickOnce()`, `step()`, `statusFor()`), `backend/v2/networth/stash-client.mjs` (the folder-tab path and its retries), `docs/admin.md`, "When the refresher goes quiet".
- **Tests:** `networth/folder-walk.test.mjs`, "step by step and all at once send exactly the same requests": the tick-driven walk and `walkFolderTab()`'s loop send identical request sequences and return the same items, which is the safety argument (the request count does not change: 1+N, in the same order). `networth/refresher.test.mjs`, section "A FOLDER TAB IS MANY STEPS, NOT ONE": "a folder tab costs one request per tick, and exactly 1+N in total".
- **Status:** fixed. ## B16 — The derived stash pace was a one-way street (fixed)

- **Seen:** production, 12.–13.08.2026, the rule-set log of one account on one address:
  ```
  12.08. 14:12 UTC   Account 30/60s ban 60s, Account 100/1800s ban 600s,
                     Ip 45/60s ban 120s,     Ip 180/1800s ban 600s      -> 24s spacing
  12.08. 14:13 UTC   Account 15/10s ban 60s, Account 30/300s ban 300s   (the folder endpoint)
  13.08. 02:15 UTC   Ip 30/60s ban 120s, Ip 90/1800s ban 600s,

  13.08. 11:28 UTC   the 14:12 set again                                -> still 80s
  ```
- **Cause:** `stash-pacer.mjs` keyed its rule table on `` `${rule}:${windowSec}` `` and never removed anything, so one strict answer held the tool at 80 s for the rest of the day; only a restart cleared it, and a new address did not. The 02:15 set was an **unauthenticated** request, not a penalty: the legacy stash endpoint answers the same policy in two forms, `Account,Ip` when the POESESSID was accepted and `Ip`-only when it was not, with the address limit roughly halved (sources: the `gerwaric/acquisition` project measured it on this endpoint on 28.07.2026; a GGG web developer described it in the official forum in 2018). Nothing was tightened on suspicion: the authenticated set is digit for digit what a third party measured on 18.07.2026 from another account, address and continent, and GGG's penalties are binary (a timed lock, a withdrawn credential, a Cloudflare block). No policy header → no replacement, the set joins the union (a missing header must not make two endpoints look like one budget). `POE_STASH_RULE_TTL_MS` (6 h) expires a set nobody asks for; its floor is 2 h (the longest window observed), each rule is kept at least for its own window, and since the dangerous direction is DOWN, a refused value falls back up. The refusal already travels the auth path: 401/403 → `kind:'auth'` → the user is paused, `cookie expired for <user> — renew it on the settings page (Sync settings) or in networth-users.json` is logged and the sync status reads `expired`; the Net Worth and Settings pages say so in their sync line, History and Price Check in the shared status line. An entry for a language this release does not support is still dropped, whoever it belongs to.
- **Tests:** `user-lang-store.test.mjs` ("a user taken out of POE_USERS keeps his stored language in the file", "an unsupported language of a user not in POE_USERS is still dropped"), both failed before; `backend/v2/user-lang-endpoint.test.mjs` checks the same against a started server (after a switch of player1).
- **Status:** fixed 2026-11-03.

## B34 — The price brake forgot names after a failed sidecar write (fixed)

- **Seen:** found in the follow-up to B31, 2026-11-03; reproduced in a restart test, not observed in production.
- **Cause:** after a recorded point `networth/history.mjs` writes the items sidecar (`networth-history-items.json`) and then the price brake's memory (`networth-history-guard.json`); the memory leaves out the names the newest point carries with a price, because they come back from the sidecar. `save()` answered false on a write error, without a log line, and nobody read it. The sidecar on disk then still held the point before, was not anchored on the newest point at the next start and was not used, so those prices were gone and the brake let a jump through (in the test: a card remembered at 1c entered at 60c). At a `tabsChanged` with an already lagging sidecar the rule took other users' names out again. The gap is older than B31.
- **Fix:** (`networth/history.mjs`, `history-items.mjs`) the memory leaves names to the sidecar only when the sidecar write of that same moment returned true. After a failed write it carries every name, at both places; it is also written when the sidecar save throws (after a point the error goes on to the caller), and the next successful sidecar write leaves names to it again. A failed sidecar write is logged once per error code: `[networth-history-items] could not write … (<code>) — the file keeps its previous content; …`.
- **Tests:** `networth/history-restart.test.mjs` ("a sidecar write that fails after a point does not take names out of the guard memory", "a sidecar write that fails after a change of tabs does not take names out of the guard memory"; the tmp write fails through a test seam, the card stays at 1c; both failed with 159 instead of 100 before); `networth/history-items.test.mjs` ("a failed write answers false and leaves the previous file as it was", with no tmp file left; "a failed write is logged once per error code").
- **Status:** fixed 2026-11-03.

## B35 — A breakdown dropped at a change of stash tabs came back after a restart when the sidecar write failed (fixed)

- **Seen:** found in the review of B34, 2026-11-03; reproduced in a test outside the repo (`latest('player1')` returned the old breakdown), not observed in production.
- **Cause:** at a `tabsChanged` `history.mjs` drops the user's breakdown in memory and in the sidecar and rewrites the sidecar. When that write failed, the old sidecar's entry was still anchored on the user's newest point (the only test at start), so a restart adopted the dropped breakdown: the first point after it diffed across the change of tabs, and the brake judged against it. The gap lasted until a later successful sidecar write; it is older than B34.
- **Fix:** (`history.mjs`, `guard-state.mjs`) `networth-history-guard.json`, written right after the sidecar, carries an optional field `stale` (the user and anchor of the entry left on disk; no new schema version, an older release ignores it). The next start reads it before the sidecar and leaves that entry out, with the line `[networth-history-items] not adopting the breakdown of "<user>" … a change of stash tabs dropped it before the restart and the write that should have removed it failed`; the first point after the restart carries no delta. The mark is kept while the entry is on disk and ends with the next sidecar write that lands.
- **Tests:** `networth/history-restart.test.mjs` ("a breakdown dropped at a change of tabs stays dropped when the sidecar write there failed": `latest('player1')` null, player2 adopted, no delta, the mark gone after a landed write; "without a failed write at the change of tabs nothing is marked stale"; "a sidecar write that lands at a later change of tabs clears the stale mark"). Checked by mutation.
- **Status:** fixed 2026-11-03. Not covered: when the brake's memory write fails as well (a full disk), when the memory file is left untouched (a newer release's, or unreadable), or after a rollback to a release without the fix — the next start then adopts the dropped breakdown again.

## B36 — An exchange line at exactly 10 units failed the 10-unit floor (fixed)

- **Seen:** found 2026-11-03 in the review of the market fixtures, by running the valuator over the raw overviews of that day's recording; not observed as a wrong price in production. Jagged Fossil in the 2026-11-03 recording (`exchange-Fossil.json`, raw only): `primaryValue` 20.01, `volumePrimaryValue` 200.1, unpriced.
- **Cause:** `exchangeLineValue` in `networth/valuator.mjs` counts units as `volumePrimaryValue / primaryValue` and prices a line when that is at least 10 (`MIN_EXCHANGE_UNITS`) or when it clears the 2000 chaos turnover rescue. 200.1 / 20.01 gives 9.999999999999998 in floating point, so a line of exactly 10 units by its recorded numbers failed. A second, rounding half: the recording carries both fields with at most 2 decimals between 1 and 100 (1 decimal from 100 to 1000, none above), so a count near 10 is good to about 0.01; Deafening Essence of Torment (5.92 and 59.17) counts 9.995 units, which could be 9.986 to 10.004 before rounding. Of the 641 exchange lines with both numbers, these two are the only ones between 9.9 and 10.1 units; Faustus priced both (Jagged Fossil 6.5c, the essence 4.1c), and the floor decides only where Faustus has no price.
- **Fix:** `reachesFloor` in `sell-side.mjs` lets a value that misses a floor by at most a billionth of it reach it (`FLOAT_SLACK`, 1e-9 of the floor). It serves both unit floors of `exchangeLineValue` (10 units, and the one whole trade behind the turnover rescue, where it never acts), the 2x disagreement of the cross-hour check in `networth/faustus.mjs` (1.9999999999999996), and the takeover and hold bars of the sell-side comparison in `sell-side.mjs` (a 5 % edge computing to 4.999999999999982 %). Three quotient comparisons in `faustus.mjs` do not use it: the band check of `volumeRateXperQuote` (quotients of whole numbers, no error) and the two lower edges of the divine-bridge envelope (`env.min / BRIDGE_TOLERANCE`, divide by 4) — a plausibility band whose tolerance is measured in coarse steps (4 against 3 and 2 on one bridge path, against 1.5 and no envelope on the other; comments in `buildDetailById`). The rounding half gets no allowance, by decision: Deafening Essence of Torment at 9.995 units stays unpriced, because a rejected line falls back to the next source and a wrongly priced one does not.
- **Tests:** `networth/valuator.test.mjs` ("exactly 10 units by the stated numbers are a price, though the division gives 9.999999999999998", with 9.99 still rejected; "a count a hundred-millionth of the floor short by the stated numbers stays rejected", 9.9999999 units, which fails with a slack of 1e-8 or more); `networth/recorded-thresholds.test.mjs` ("exchange floor at 10 units: Jagged Fossil at exactly 10 units is a price, 9.995 units are not"); `networth/faustus.test.mjs` ("two rates exactly 2x apart by their numbers trigger the cross-hour check, though the division gives a hair under 2"); `sell-side.test.mjs` ("an edge of exactly CHAOS_TAKEOVER_PCT by the numbers takes over, though the division gives a hair under it"). All four failed before and fail with a slack of 0; the two exchange-floor tests also fail with a slack of 1e-3, which would let rounding through.
- **Status:** fixed 2026-11-03, the floating-point half; the rounding half stays on purpose.
